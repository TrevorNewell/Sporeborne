# Sporeborne Style Guide — Rootways Zone

Grounded entirely in `design/GDD_Sporeborne.md`. Scope for Assignment #7: hub-NPC
dialogue and flavor text (the same content class Assignment #4's `WarrenDialogue`
and `BestiaryFlavor`/`SporeFlavor` agents generate). Three distinct, checkable rules:

## Rule 1 — Tone: cozy above, deadly below

> "Cozy above, deadly below — the warm Warren makes the dark descent land. Contrast
> is the tone." (GDD §1)
> "Cute on the surface, dark underneath — Binding of Isaac's tonal range with Dead
> Cells' movement feel." (GDD §1)

Dialogue and flavor text must carry warmth **and** an undercurrent of real danger at
the same time — never one without the other. Fails this rule if it reads as:
- Generic cartoon-shopkeeper cheer with no edge (excess exclamation points, empty
  enthusiasm, no acknowledgment that the Rootways are actually dangerous), **or**
- Flat grimdark misery with no warmth at all (no fondness, no home-ness to the
  Warren, nothing cozy).

## Rule 2 — Vocabulary/lore accuracy: Gold vs. Bloom, Snail vs. Beetle

> "Two currencies, cleanly separated so they never compete: Gold is in-run only
> (spent at Snail's shop, gone on death) and Bloom is persistent (earned per run,
> spent at Beetle's Spore Archive)." (GDD §3)

**Gold** belongs to **Snail's shop** (in-run, lost on death). **Bloom** belongs to
**Beetle's Spore Archive** (persistent, meta-progression). A line that has Beetle
talk about Gold/"my shop," or Snail talk about Bloom/"the Archive," is a lore error
— this exact class of mistake was already caught for real once, in Assignment #4's
live Critic Agent run (Snail credited with Beetle's Spore Archive).

## Rule 3 — Formatting/length: hub-NPC lines are short spoken barks

> "Generates ≤3 hub-NPC lines from a run summary... capped." (GDD §8, Warren Voices)

Hub-NPC dialogue is a short spoken line or two — not a monologue, not narration, no
asterisked stage directions, no multi-paragraph prose. Roughly 1–2 sentences,
plain spoken text only.

## Grading

Grade 1–10 against all three rules together. A 10 means all three are satisfied
simultaneously; a violation of any one rule caps the score well below 10, scaled to
how many rules are broken and how badly.
